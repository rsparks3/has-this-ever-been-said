Assumptions you can change

How much language has humanity produced? Nobody knows precisely, so these are order-of-magnitude defaults. Edit them and the results update instantly.

Defaults: about 117 billion people have ever been born (Population Reference Bureau); people speak roughly 16,000 words a day (Mehl et al., 2007); historical life expectancy keeps the average speaking lifetime short. Writing is dominated by email and chat, around 1017 words; every book ever printed is only about 1013. The indexed web is roughly 1015 words.

How the estimate works

P(text) = Π P(tokeni | tokens before it) from the language model
expected prior occurrences λ = N × P(text) N = words ever produced in that scope
P(said before) = 1 − e−λ

Treats every position in all of human language as an independent draw from the model's distribution. The model (not loaded yet) learned from written web text, so it under-rates how repetitive speech is: greetings, song lyrics and stock phrases are said far more often than it thinks. It also treats capitalization and punctuation variants as different texts. Read the results as "roughly how many orders of magnitude", not as a measurement.
